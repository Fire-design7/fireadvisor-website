Active systems — fire detection, fire suppression, smoke and heat control — are triggered by a fire. Passive protection is permanent: it is in the walls, floors, doors and seals, and it limits the spread of fire. Below are the requirements of the regulations and the kinds of products used to meet them — with examples from Protecta's products.

**In short**

- Fire barriers **prevent the spread of fire and of the products of combustion** to adjacent rooms.
- Fire compartment barriers have a minimum fire resistance of **REI (EI) 120**, and the doors in them — **EI 90**.
- Every **opening for pipes, cables and other services** and every **linear joint** through a barrier is sealed without reducing the fire resistance of the barrier.
- Sealing systems have a **fire resistance class under two-sided fire exposure**.
- **Fire doors** are checked at least once a year.

## How fire resistance is measured

Fire resistance is assessed by three criteria:

- **R — load-bearing capacity:** the ability of a structure to keep its stability under fire from one or more sides for a given time;
- **E — integrity:** the ability of an element to stop flames and hot gases from passing to the unexposed side;
- **I — insulation:** the ability to prevent intense heat transfer to the unexposed side, so that nothing close to it ignites.

Load-bearing elements require R, load-bearing separating elements — R, E and I, and non-load-bearing separating elements — E and I. That is why you see **REI** and **EI**, followed by minutes — for example EI 90.

Elements, structures and installations are classified under the **BDS EN 13501** series of standards. Fire resistance is determined on the basis of **tests, calculations or comparisons** with tables.

## Fire compartments and barriers

Fire compartments divide the building so that a fire does not spread from the part where it started to the rest. Compartment barriers are:

- made of products with a reaction-to-fire class not lower than **A2**;
- of a minimum fire resistance of **REI (EI) 120**;
- with openings of a total area of up to 25% of the vertical barrier and up to 10% of the horizontal one. Openings are protected with **self-closing doors**, hatches or closing devices of a minimum fire resistance of **EI 90**;
- with non-opening glazing of fire resistance **EI 120**. Openable windows in them are not permitted.

The vertical barriers of fire compartments on the top floor are designed to continue in an unbroken vertical plane through the roof space. An example of a product for compartmenting roof cavities: **Protecta FR Membrane Barrier**.

## Sealing openings and joints

Pipes, cables, ducts and cable trays pass through walls and floors. The openings through which they pass **are sealed without reducing the normative fire resistance of the barrier**. The same applies to **linear joints** crossing fire barriers.

What to keep in mind:

- **System class.** Systems sealing openings and linear joints in fire barriers (except horizontal ones) have a fire resistance class under **two-sided** fire exposure. The requirement is for the sealing **system**, not only for the product.
- **Reaction to fire.** Products used to seal openings have a reaction-to-fire class not lower than **A2** in buildings of fire resistance degrees I and II and not lower than **C** in degree III buildings.
- **Pipe insulation.** Where pipes pass through fire barriers, thermal insulation is made of products of reaction-to-fire class **A1L**.
- **Ducts.** Where a duct passes through a fire barrier, its thermal insulation is interrupted with products of reaction-to-fire class not lower than **A2**.

## Fireproofing of steel structures

To ensure the fire resistance of structures, coating products — intumescent paints, compounds and claddings — and technical solutions are used, depending on the section, the number of sides heated and the section factor of the profiles.

The fire resistance of load-bearing steel elements is determined through their load-bearing capacity (R). The critical temperature of steel may be determined under the Eurocodes or taken as follows:

- **550 °C** — for elements heated on four sides with I, H and other open sections, and for circular hollow sections;
- **500 °C** — for elements heated on four sides with a rectangular hollow section;
- **600 °C** — for elements heated on three sides with I, H and other open sections.

An example of a kind of product: the acrylic steel paints **Protecta Steel Paint FR-1** and **FR-120**, which according to the manufacturer give protection for up to 60 and up to 120 minutes.

## Fire doors

Doors in fire barriers have a fire resistance class under two-sided fire exposure. Doors that are required to be self-closing meet at least class **C1** or **C3**, depending on their type.

Under Regulation № 8121з-647, the control of fire doors and smoke-tight doors is carried out by the owner (manager) of the facility or by the fire safety officers through checks **at intervals of up to 1 year**, following the manufacturer's instructions. Facilities of subclasses Ф1.3 and Ф1.4 are exempt. Checks are recorded in a **log** when the facility has more than 5 doors, and the manager of the facility is notified in writing of every fault.

## Maintenance

Facilities are kept in the technical condition in which they were put into operation. The sealing requirement applies to every opening through a fire barrier, including those made later — for a new cable, a new pipe or a new duct.
